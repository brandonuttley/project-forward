# Scope Change Memo

The artifact you send to your sponsor when scope needs to change — a deadline is slipping, a new requirement has emerged, the budget needs to expand, or you need to cut something to meet the date. The memo forces clarity on what's changing, why, and what the sponsor needs to decide. Done well, it's the difference between a controlled adjustment and a project quietly going sideways.

**When to use:** Any time the project moves outside the bounds set by the charter. Material schedule slips, budget overruns, scope additions or reductions, resource changes.

**When NOT to use:** Minor adjustments handled within the project lead's authority. (Define this in the charter — e.g., "scope changes under [X% / $X] are project lead's call.")

**Format default:** docx — this gets formal sign-off and lands in the project record.

---

# Scope Change Request: [Initiative Name]

**To:** [Sponsor name + role]
**From:** [Project Lead name + role]
**Date:** [Date]
**Re:** [One-sentence summary — e.g., "Proposing 3-week schedule extension to accommodate Compliance review"]
**Decision needed by:** [Date]
**Decision required:** [Approve / Reject / Modify]

## TL;DR

*Three sentences. What's changing. Why. What the sponsor is being asked to approve.*

[e.g., "We're requesting a 3-week schedule extension, moving launch from Sept 30 to Oct 21. The Compliance team's Q3 backlog means our review can't start until 9/12, which doesn't leave enough runway for changes. Approving the extension lets us launch with full review; rejecting it would require us to launch with conditional Compliance sign-off, which carries regulatory risk."]

## What we agreed to (the original)

*Reference the charter or the prior commitment. Quote it directly so there's no ambiguity about what's changing.*

- **Scope:** [As defined at kickoff]
- **Schedule:** [Original timeline]
- **Budget:** [Original budget]
- **Resources:** [Original team commitment]

## What changed

*Be honest about whether this was discoverable at kickoff or genuinely new. Sponsors trust PMs who own the discovery delay.*

[e.g., "When we set the timeline, we assumed Compliance review would take 5 business days based on a similar project last year. We've since learned that Compliance is operating with a Q3 backlog because of [reason], pushing standard review to 4 weeks."]

## What we're proposing

*The specific change. Be concrete.*

- **New scope:** [If changing]
- **New schedule:** [Specific dates]
- **New budget:** [Specific dollars]
- **New resource ask:** [Specific people, hours, or roles]

## Why this option (vs. alternatives)

*Show your work. The sponsor should see that you considered the obvious alternatives and rejected them for stated reasons.*

| Option | Description | Why we're not choosing this |
|---|---|---|
| Stick with original timeline | Launch on 9/30 with conditional Compliance sign-off | Carries regulatory risk; precedent for future projects |
| Cut scope to hit original date | Drop [feature X] from launch | Loses the most-requested capability; creates technical debt |
| **Extend by 3 weeks (recommended)** | Launch 10/21 with full review | Adds 3 weeks; preserves scope and reduces risk |
| Extend by 6 weeks | Launch 11/11 with conservative buffer | More than needed; pushes into Q4 lockdown period |

## Impact analysis

*What changes downstream from this decision.*

- **Customer/stakeholder impact:** [e.g., "Marketing campaign already scheduled for week of 10/1 needs to shift"]
- **Resource impact:** [e.g., "Two engineers committed to next initiative on 10/15 — need to renegotiate"]
- **Budget impact:** [e.g., "Adds $X in vendor extension fees and $Y in internal labor"]
- **Risk impact:** [e.g., "Reduces regulatory risk; introduces market timing risk if competitor launches in window"]
- **Communications impact:** [Who needs to be told, what they need to hear, when]

## What we need from you

*Specific, actionable.*

1. Approve or reject the proposal by [date]
2. If approved, please share the change with [other executives] before [date]
3. [Any other ask]

## Recommendation

*State the recommendation plainly. Sponsors appreciate a clear point of view.*

[e.g., "Recommend approval. The 3-week extension is the lowest-risk path that preserves scope, and the Q3 Compliance backlog isn't something we can engineer around."]

---

## Notes for the PM

- **Don't bury the ask.** The TL;DR and "what we need from you" are the most important sections. Some sponsors will read only those.
- **Own the discovery.** If you should have caught this at kickoff, say so. Sponsors trust PMs who acknowledge what they missed.
- **One memo, one decision.** Don't bundle three scope changes into one memo. Each gets its own.
- **Timing matters.** Send before the change is forced, not after. A scope change memo two days before launch is too late.
- **Preserve dignity in the ask.** A sponsor who feels cornered by a memo will find ways to say no. Frame as "here are options, here's our recommendation, here's what we need."
