# Weekly Status Report

The standing weekly update to stakeholders. Done well, it builds trust and surfaces problems early. Done badly, it's noise that no one reads. The discipline is to be honest, brief, and consistent — same format every week, same day every week.

**When to use:** Standing weekly cadence on any project lasting more than a few weeks. Audience is the project team plus key stakeholders (sponsor, consulted parties, sometimes executive leadership).

**When NOT to use:** A project so short that the kickoff and launch are the only milestones that matter.

**Format default:** docx or email-formatted markdown. Most teams send via email or Slack.

## Discipline rules

1. **Same format every week.** Stakeholders learn the structure and read faster.
2. **Same send day, same send time.** Predictable cadence builds trust.
3. **Be honest about red.** A status report that's always green either misrepresents reality or covers a project too small to need a status report.
4. **Don't pad.** If there's nothing in a section, write "Nothing this week." Padding breeds skimming.
5. **Link, don't paste.** Long content goes in linked artifacts (charter, RAID log, etc.). The status report is the index, not the encyclopedia.
6. **Keep under one page (or the email-equivalent).** If your status report is two pages, you're either reporting too much or running too big a program with too little structure.

## Notes for the PM

- The first 3-4 weeks of status reports will feel awkward. By week six, the rhythm is doing real work — readers know what to expect, the team knows what to capture, and problems surface earlier.
- If you're tempted to skip a week because "nothing happened," send the report anyway with "nothing material this week, on track for [next milestone]." Skipping breaks the pattern.
- Watch for the moment when status reports stop being read. That usually means either (a) the project is in trouble and stakeholders are avoiding it, or (b) the project is so quiet that you can move to biweekly. Diagnose which.

## Honesty checks before sending

The single biggest failure mode of weekly status reports is reflexive optimism. Run these checks before you click send.

- **Are you reporting green when you actually feel yellow?** If the team is anxious about something but the report says "on track," you're trading short-term comfort for long-term credibility. Sponsors notice when status reports never go yellow and then suddenly go red. Trust them with the yellow earlier.
- **Are your accomplishments outputs or activities?** "Held three vendor meetings" is activity. "Selected vendor and have draft contract in Legal review" is output. If your "what we did" section is mostly activities, the project may not be moving as much as the report suggests.
- **Are the "decisions needed" actually decisions, or just things you want sponsor awareness of?** A decision the sponsor will return to you with "thanks for the FYI" wasn't actually a decision needed. Be precise.
- **What did you decide not to put in this report, and why?** If you're filtering out something material because it's politically inconvenient or you're hoping to resolve it before next week, that's the most important entry — put it in.
- **Would your sponsor be surprised to learn anything you're omitting?** Surprise three weeks from now is more expensive than discomfort today.
