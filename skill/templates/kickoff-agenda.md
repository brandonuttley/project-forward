# Kickoff Meeting Agenda

The first cross-functional meeting on a complex initiative. Done well, it sets the tone for the whole project. Done badly, it creates ambiguity that takes weeks to clean up. The kickoff is not a status meeting or a brainstorm — it's a structured alignment.

**When to use:** Beginning of any cross-functional initiative spanning multiple teams or weeks.

**When NOT to use:** A second meeting on a project that already has shared context. That's a working session, not a kickoff.

**Format default:** docx for the formal agenda sent in advance. Markdown for working notes.

## Agenda

### 1. Welcome and context (5 min)
*Sponsor opens. Why this matters to the business. Why now.*

### 2. What "done" looks like (10 min)
*Project Lead walks through the success criteria from the charter. Confirm shared understanding. Surface any disagreement now — this is the cheapest moment to resolve it.*

### 3. Scope: what's in, what's out (10 min)
*Walk through the in-scope deliverables and the explicit out-of-scope items. Push back is welcome here. Lock it down.*

### 4. Stakeholder map walkthrough (10 min)
*Confirm sponsor, owners, and consulted parties. Surface any role ambiguity. Confirm Legal/Compliance/Security engagement plan and lead times.*

### 5. Top three risks (10 min)
*Brief discussion of the highest-priority risks from the RAID log. Owners confirm mitigations. Add any new risks the room raises.*

### 6. Working cadence (10 min)
*Confirm:*
- Weekly status meeting [day/time/format]
- Steering committee cadence [if applicable]
- Working channel (Slack, Teams, etc.)
- Where the artifacts live (shared drive, Confluence, Workfront, etc.)
- Decision log and RAID log location

### 7. First two weeks: what happens next (10 min)
*Specific commitments by name. Not "we'll get started" — "Sarah will deliver the technical design doc by Friday. Marcus will schedule the Legal intake by Wednesday."*

### 8. Questions, open issues, concerns (10 min)
*Explicitly invite friction. Easier to surface now than to dig out later.*

*Sponsor restates commitment. Names what they expect from the team. Confirms availability for escalations.*

## Facilitator notes

- **Send the pre-read 48 hours ahead.** People who haven't read won't engage well. Push the meeting if pre-reads aren't ready.
- **Don't run a status meeting.** This isn't "where are we?" — there's nothing to update. It's "what are we doing and how?"
- **Capture decisions live.** Type into the decision log as the meeting happens. By the end, decisions are recorded, not waiting on a summary email.
- **Watch for the silent disagreer.** Someone nodding without speaking is often the person who'll surprise you in week three. Call on people directly — "Maria, this affects your team most. Anything you'd push back on?"
- **End with explicit next steps.** Every action has a name and a date. If a vague "we'll figure that out" emerges, name who owns figuring it out and when.

## Common kickoff failure modes

- **Sponsor doesn't show up.** Reschedule. Don't kickoff without them.
- **Too many people in the room.** A 25-person kickoff is a press conference. Cut to the people who'll actually do the work, plus required executives.
- **Treating it as a celebration.** Kickoffs are for alignment, not enthusiasm. Save the celebration for the launch.
- **No follow-up artifacts.** A kickoff with no decisions logged, no actions assigned, no scheduled cadence is just a meeting. Send the recap within 24 hours.
