# Marketing Campaign Brief

A one-to-two page document that defines a marketing campaign before any creative work, media buying, or production starts. Forces the team to agree on audience, message, success metrics, and constraints up front. The marketing equivalent of a project charter — and the artifact that prevents the most common marketing failure mode (creative misaligned with strategy).

**When to use:** Before kicking off creative or production for any campaign with material spend, multiple workstreams, or executive visibility.

**When NOT to use:** Single-asset, single-channel work that's part of an already-briefed campaign. A new social post under an existing campaign brief doesn't need its own brief.

**Format default:** docx for distribution and sign-off. Markdown for the working draft.

## Notes for the marketing PM

- **The brief is the contract.** Every disagreement that emerges later — about creative direction, channel mix, success criteria — should resolve back to what's written here. If the brief is silent on something important, the team will discover that in week three when it's expensive to fix.
- **One brief per campaign.** Don't bundle two unrelated efforts into one brief just because they share a budget line. Separate briefs make for separate accountability.
- **Get the audience section right.** The most common brief failure is a vague audience definition. "Marketing decision-makers" isn't an audience. "VP-level marketing leaders at fintech companies with 100-500 employees evaluating an analytics platform in the next 6 months" is.
- **Approve in writing.** Verbal approval doesn't count. The approver signs the brief. That signature matters when scope shifts in week six.
- **Update when reality changes.** A stale brief is dangerous. When budget changes, audience refines, or success criteria shift, update the brief and re-approve.
